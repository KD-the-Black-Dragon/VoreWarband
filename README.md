# Vore Warband
This game is a continuation of darkevilme's game Vore War. It is a turn based tactical game with rpg elements in a vore related world.

Check out [The Eka's thread](https://aryion.com/forum/viewtopic.php?f=79&t=65398) by @Flame_Valxsarion for more information on the original Vore War! 

This fork is developing a mercenary-company total conversion. Completed fork changes are recorded with dates in [CHANGES.md](CHANGES.md); planned work is described in the [conversion roadmap](docs/conversion-roadmap.md). The applicable GNU GPL version 3 terms are included in [License.txt](License.txt), and existing upstream attribution and notices are retained.

## Play the company proof of concept

Open the project in Unity 2018.3.12f1, open `Assets/base.unity`, enter Play mode and select **New Campaign**. Create your leader, hire a couple of starters in the town, equip them and take local contracts. **Help** opens the shared blue campaign guide, with compact chapters and a scrollable reading area. New Campaign offers company creation and Load Game without the retired Continue/map-preview entries. Use the persistent **Contracts / Company / Map / Town / Records** buttons to switch views. Drag the centered map with the left or middle mouse button; **Center company** brings the company back into view. New regions are120x80tiles with six zones (three columns/two rows), one town per zone and a bottom-center start. Towns form a two-exit road circuit, with28–34road moves between connected towns (about five six-action days). New companies provisionally have six daily actions; existing saves keep their map dimensions and stored allowance. Roads clear to one-action grassland, with original farming fields on adjacent non-road town tiles. Campaign leader revival is available in normal **Options**. Options groups preferences into one centered, scrollable blue panel; the Campaign category appears only when a company is active. Content Settings uses the same blue styling, with its page dropdown at the top and a scrollable column on each page. Town services stay in the left column, and ready members can level up directly from the roster. Rest requires a full daily action budget; **End turn** remains separate. **Single Battle** opens the separate tactical setup, with core controls at the top and Attackers/Defenders in independently scrollable columns with species previews. **Load Game** on the main menu opens the company Save / Load browser. Traveling outside towns can now trigger wild Spitter Slug combat or a practice-duel request from healthy mercenaries.

Company Save / Load supports named manual slots, lightweight progress previews, confirmed deletion and three recovery backups per slot. Autosaves are queued in the background at campaign-day and player-battle-turn starts. Ordinary movement, purchases and hiring do not autosave. Explicit Save/Quicksave requests also use the worker. The browser reads summaries in the background and reuses them for paging and hovering. Losing progress since the last completed save is an accepted recovery limit. Normal company battles can be saved while ready to act on your own turn and resume that exact battlefield. Ironman uses its latest valid autosave and invalidates/removes its saves when the company ends. Original empire saves remain unsupported. Characters now use STR, AGI, INT and VOR. Species base AP is fixed at2 for Avians/Lizards/Spitter Slugs and3 for Gnolls; weapon misses at51%+ accuracy get one same-chance graze retry, while initial hits can critically hit based on INT. Ordinary movement next to an enemy now provokes a free melee opportunity attack before each step, including from enemies with no AP left. Orange path steps warn about reactions; reactions commit Undo. Enemy AI compares equipped melee, ranged and vore options, and can spend movement AP to reach an attack while keeping the final AP for it. Ranged units can also reposition for safer spacing when worthwhile. Opportunity-attack risk is considered, while unarmed sapient reactions are discounted during AI planning only; they still occur normally in combat. Baseline combat traits now have exclusive I–III tiers. Body-part injuries use Bruised/Battered/Mangled severity; lasting complications cap at tier III. Low current HP adds Light/Moderate/Severe Damage penalties, and chest injuries restrict healing without removing existing HP. Balance remains provisional. See the [integrated review](docs/implementation/12-integrated-review-and-help.md) and [release-readiness record](docs/release-readiness.md) for performed checks, pending graphical player/combat coverage and inherited provenance/distribution gates. No release is declared by this proof of concept.


Merchants offer three provisional gear tiers at reputation 0/10/25, with the same unlimited gear stock in every town and slot filters. Spellbooks are a separate rotating town selection: each available category offers one book from its highest unlocked tier, with a chance of one extra book, and the selection refreshes on payday. Provisional spell tiers unlock at reputation 0/10/25/50; generated enemies receive spells according to their level rather than player reputation. Hiring quotes include equipment, prorated advance wages and debt. New companies start with 260 gold to afford two starter hires; existing campaigns keep their treasury. Company upkeep starts at 40 gold on the next payday and rises by 10 each following week. Unpaid upkeep must be explicitly cleared before the next payday to avoid critical insolvency. Existing campaigns start with the initial bill at their next scheduled payday, with no back billing. Existing offer/accepted reward quotes and personal debts are preserved, while new rating-1 offers pay 220–260 gold. Level-up choices preview the next weekly wage and total company bills before commitment.

New campaign content now has a provisional calendar difficulty ramp: the first 21 enrolled days are protected, then newly refreshed contract offers and newly generated Spitter Slug road encounters gain increasing pressure. Contract ratings keep their fixed force/reward meanings, already accepted contracts stay unchanged, and older campaigns enroll at their current day instead of receiving a sudden late-game jump. Exact pacing is pending grouped player review; see the [40F implementation notes](docs/implementation/40f-calendar-difficulty.md).

Developer testing: the main-menu **Battle Lab** beside Race Editor uses the same project-only password. It opens an isolated two-unit scenario with trait/gear/injury/resource controls, native combat and recovery actions, seeded resets and in-memory fixtures. The **Power** page explains healthy capability separately from current wounds; scoring weights remain provisional. It does not edit campaign saves; closing restores prior settings and RNG.

### Species balance authoring

Open **Race Editor**, edit an explicit developer draft and use **Balance workflow** to save/load drafts, review/import packages or export approved settings. The active draft is identified on the main menu and in Battle Lab. **Use official defaults** leaves draft mode. New individuals use the selected balance, while existing company characters retain their saved species settings.

To ship approved edits, open Unity's **Warband > Balance > Import and promote** window, choose the exported package and review the changes. Promotion updates packaged data and prepares its dated change records. Restart Play mode or build again to use the new official defaults. The initial package preserves the retained pre-40A settings. Global trait formulas and new abilities still require code changes.

Keep the whole folder together, including VoreWar_Data, UnityPlayer.dll and MonoBleedingEdge. The older MVP-Review build predates the current UI/rendering changes.

To make a later build yourself in Unity2018: stop Play mode, choose **File > Build Settings**, select **PC, Mac & Linux Standalone**, **Windows**, **x86_64**, and ensure **Assets/base.unity** is checked in Scenes In Build. Choose **Build** (or **Build And Run**) and select a new folder under this project's Builds directory. The optional MultiBuild preset uses the project-relative Builds folder. Standard Build Settings remembers its own last output location. New Warband builds use their own Vore Warband player data and preferences instead of the VoreWar location. Building does not publish the game. The current private build includes license/notices; release/distribution reviews remain separate.
